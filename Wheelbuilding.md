Throughout my project, I made a few interesting decisions. Building and truing my own wheels was one of them. It started when I saw a deal for a DT Swiss CR1600 Spline 23 Rear wheel for $60. This wheel is a discontinued model that I had my sights set on because of one thing. It had the old DT Swiss 350 hubs. The rear hub alone costs at least 120 dollars, and the spokes are straight pull-bladed spokes. Though the rim was completely messed up, I had full confidence that I could bring this wheel back to life. 

On thing to consider is that components may not always have the exact model listed on it, particularly when it comes to OEM or older models. When I went out to buy this wheel, I didn't check to see what the older DT Swiss hubs looked like, and rejected the offer thinking that it was a phony DT Swiss hub. After searching it up I realized hat it was actually a genuine DT Swiss 350 hub, but because it was the older OEM model, it didn't show. Luckily, after doing some confirming and checking online, the seller was willing to come back out and complete the transaction.

When it comes to wheel building, there are a few important specs that are important to consider: 1) Effective rim diameter (ERD), 2) spoke hole count, 3) rim size, 4) rim type.
